SAYS Bake when the starter is at or just past its peak, usually 4–8 hours after feeding at room temperature (about 75°F / 24°C).

- **Feed ratio matters.** At 1:1:1 (starter:flour:water) it peaks in about 4–6 hours. At 1:5:5 it takes closer to 8–12.
- **Signs it's ready.** It has doubled or more, the top is domed or just starting to flatten, it's full of bubbles, and it smells tangy and yeasty. A spoonful should float in water, though that test isn't foolproof.
- **Don't wait for the collapse.** If the top has sunk and the surface is wrinkled, it's past peak. It will still work, but the rise will be weaker and the flavor sourer.
- **Cooler kitchen:** expect it to take longer. In a Tacoma November, a starter by a cold window may need 8–10 hours at 1:1:1.

If you feed it on a schedule, you can learn its timing by marking the jar with a rubber band at the starting level and watching when it peaks.
