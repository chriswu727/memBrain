SAYS The main thing is to keep the pan dry and keep a thin layer of seasoning on it.

- **Dry it right away.** After washing, towel it off, then set it on a low burner for a minute or two until all the moisture is gone. Air-drying is how most pans rust.
- **Oil it lightly after each use.** While the pan is still warm, rub in a few drops of a neutral oil (canola, grapeseed, or vegetable) and wipe off the excess until it looks almost dry. Too much oil turns sticky.
- **Wash gently.** Hot water and a brush or scraper are enough. A little mild soap is fine. Just don't soak the pan or put it in the dishwasher.
- **Store it somewhere dry.** If you stack pans, put a paper towel inside to absorb humidity.
- **Cook with fat often.** Regular use builds the seasoning. Long simmers of acidic foods like tomato sauce can wear it down, so re-oil afterward.

If rust shows up, scrub it off with steel wool or a scouring pad. Then dry the pan and re-season it. Rub on a thin coat of oil, wipe it nearly all off, and bake it upside down at about 450–500°F (230–260°C) for an hour. The pan is usually fine after that.

SAYS The difference comes down to when you pay tax on the money.

**Traditional IRA**
- You may get a tax break now. Contributions can be deducted from this year's income, depending on your income and whether you have a workplace plan.
- The money grows tax-deferred.
- You pay regular income tax when you withdraw it in retirement.

**Roth IRA**
- There's no tax break now. You contribute money you've already paid tax on.
- The money grows tax-free.
- Qualified withdrawals in retirement, including the growth, are tax-free.

**Other differences**
- **Early withdrawals:** with a Roth, you can take out what you contributed at any time without tax or penalty. The earnings are the part that's restricted. A traditional IRA usually penalizes early withdrawals of any kind.
- **Required withdrawals:** a traditional IRA forces you to start taking money out at a set age. A Roth doesn't during your lifetime.
- **Income limits:** high earners can't contribute directly to a Roth. Deducting traditional contributions also phases out at certain incomes if you have a workplace plan.
- **Contribution limit:** the annual cap is the same, and it's shared between the two types. The limit changes by year, so check the current figure.

**Rule of thumb**
- If you expect to be in a higher tax bracket later, a Roth usually comes out ahead.
- If you expect to be in a lower bracket in retirement, the traditional IRA's upfront deduction is usually better.
- If you're unsure, many people split their contributions between the two.

This is general information, not tax advice. A tax professional can help if your situation is complicated.

SAYS Here are some things that tend to help when your mind won't slow down:

- **Get the thoughts out of your head.** Keep a notepad by the bed and spend a few minutes writing down worries, to-dos, and anything you're afraid of forgetting. Once it's on paper, your brain has less reason to keep rehearsing it.
- **Slow your breathing.** Breathe in for 4 counts and out for 6 to 8. A longer exhale nudges your body toward calm. Do about 10 rounds.
- **Give your mind a boring task.** Try counting backward from 300 by 3s, or picking a category and naming an item for each letter of the alphabet. This works better than trying to "think about nothing."
- **Relax your body in sequence.** Tense each muscle group for a few seconds, then release it, starting at your feet and working up.
- **Don't lie there fighting it.** If you're still awake after about 20 minutes, get up and do something dim and dull, like reading a paper book or stretching. Go back to bed when you feel drowsy. This keeps your bed linked to sleep rather than frustration.
- **Set up the evening.** Dim the lights and put screens away for the last 30 to 60 minutes. Schedule a "worry window" earlier in the evening, and cut caffeine by early afternoon.

If racing thoughts keep you up most nights for weeks, it's worth talking to a doctor. CBT-I, a structured therapy for insomnia, works well.
