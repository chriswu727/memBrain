SAYS Keep a thin layer of polymerized oil on the pan, and never let it stay wet.

- **Dry it right away.** After washing, towel it off, then set it on a low burner for a minute or two to drive out moisture from the pores.
- **Oil it while it's warm.** Rub in a very thin coat of a neutral oil (grapeseed, canola, or flaxseed), then wipe it off as if you'd made a mistake. A thick layer turns sticky.
- **Re-season occasionally.** Bake the pan upside down at about 450–475°F (230–245°C) for an hour with a thin oil coat. This builds the protective layer that blocks rust.
- **Avoid soaking and the dishwasher.** Standing water and long soaks are the usual causes. Mild soap and a quick wash are fine.
- **Go easy on acidic foods.** Tomato sauce and wine reductions can wear down the seasoning, especially in a young pan.
- **Store it somewhere dry.** If the air is humid, put a paper towel inside to absorb moisture. Don't stack other pans on it, and don't seal it in a lid.

If rust does appear, scrub it off with steel wool, then wash, dry, and re-season. The pan will be fine.

SAYS Here are a few things that tend to help when your mind won't slow down:

- **Write it out.** Spend 5 minutes before bed dumping thoughts, worries and tomorrow's to-dos onto paper. Your brain doesn't have to keep holding them.
- **Slow your exhale.** Breathe in for 4 and out for 6 to 8, for a few minutes. A longer exhale nudges your body toward calm.
- **Give your mind a gentle task.** Try naming an animal for each letter of the alphabet, or imagine walking through a place you love in detail. This crowds out the loops without needing effort.
- **Relax your body in sequence.** Tense and release each muscle group from your toes upward.

Don't try to force sleep. Trying hard usually keeps you awake, so aim for resting quietly instead. If a racing mind keeps you up most nights for several weeks, it's worth talking to a doctor. CBT-I, a type of therapy for insomnia, works very well.

SAYS The half-kneeling hip flexor stretch is a good one for this.

1. Kneel on one knee, on a cushion or folded towel if the floor is hard. Put the other foot flat in front of you, with that knee over your ankle.
2. Tuck your pelvis under slightly, as if you were zipping up a tight pair of pants. Squeeze the glute of the kneeling leg.

You should feel a stretch, not sharp or pinching pain. If the tightness doesn't ease after a couple of weeks, a physical therapist can check whether something else is going on.
