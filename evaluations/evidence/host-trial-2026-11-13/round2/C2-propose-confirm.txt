SAYS Good news: rust on cast iron is mostly a moisture problem, and it's easy to prevent. 🍳

- **Dry it right away.** After washing, towel it off, then set it on a burner for a minute or two to drive out the water in the pores. Don't let it air-dry.
- **Oil it thinly after each wash.** Rub in a few drops of a neutral oil (canola, grapeseed, flaxseed or vegetable), then wipe it off until the pan looks almost dry. Too much oil turns sticky.
- **Season it now and then.** Coat the pan in a thin layer of oil and bake it upside down at about 450–500°F for an hour. This builds the polymerized layer that keeps water out.
- **Keep it out of water.** Don't soak it or leave it in the sink, and keep it out of the dishwasher. Soap is fine in moderation, but rinse and dry quickly.
- **Store it somewhere dry.** If you stack pans, put a paper towel between them. Don't store it with a tight lid on, because that traps moisture. This matters in a damp Pacific Northwest kitchen.
- **Be careful with acidic food.** Long-simmered tomato sauce or wine can strip the seasoning, so re-oil after cooking them.

If rust does show up, scrub it off with steel wool, wash, dry on the burner, and re-season. The pan will be fine. 💪
